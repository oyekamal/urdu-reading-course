## 2. Synthesised RECOMMENDED Per-Letter Lesson (Urdu app)

Design rationale: this blends (a) Duolingo's tap/build/trace exercise triad [1][2], (b) Alif Baa's
"same unit teaches the letter in all its forms with explicit stroke-direction arrows" approach [6][8]
rather than Qaida's fully-deferred joining lesson, (c) Buruj Academy's letter-grouping-by-shape-family
pacing and "harakat immediately after the first letters, not after all 28" recommendation [10], and
(d) the gradual-release "I do / we do / you do" scaffold [11], which none of the reading-course
sources make explicit but is the standard model for skill acquisition and matches Duolingo's own
"model → do it with hints → do it alone" exercise ordering pattern.

**Screen-by-screen for ONE new letter (target: 3–5 min total, matches Duolingo's documented
lesson-length norm of ~5 min / 15–25 items [12][2]):**

| # | Screen | Purpose | Drill type | Pass rule | Supporting citation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sound intro** — large isolated letter + audio of its sound, "I do" | Anchor grapheme→phoneme before any interaction | Passive: tap play, letter animates/highlights | Auto-advance (no fail state — matches gradual-release "I do" phase where teacher/app models, no student output required yet) | [11] gradual release "I do" phase; [2] Duolingo reading-tab "few at a time" pacing |
| 2 | **Tap-the-sound** — 2-3 letter choices, audio plays, pick the letter that matches | Confirm recognition, "we do" (scaffolded — few distractors) | Multiple-choice tap | ≥1 correct of first 2 attempts to proceed; wrong answer replays audio + highlights correct letter | [2] Duolingo: "What sound does this make?" exercise, confirmed exercise type |
| 3 | **Select-the-letter among distractors from the same shape-family** (e.g. ب vs ت vs ث) | Force fine visual discrimination on the hardest confusion set, not random distractors | Multiple-choice tap, distractors always same dot-family | Same as above | [10] Buruj Academy's shape-family grouping (ب ت ث / ج ح خ / د ذ / ر ز / س ش) — directly informs which distractors to use |
| 4 | **Build-the-letter** — drag component strokes/tiles into place to assemble the character | Move from recognition to construction before handwriting | Drag-and-drop tiles (2-4 pieces) | Any order allowed for now; snap-to-place with haptic/visual confirm | [2] Duolingo "character building" exercise — drag partial-character tiles |
| 5 | **Trace** — finger-trace the letter over a directional-arrow guide, "body first, dots last" | Motor encoding, correct stroke direction | Stroke-tracking trace, arrows shown throughout (not narrated once and hidden) | Must stay within tolerance on ≥70% of the stroke path (typical tracing-app leniency); dot traced LAST as a separate final step | [8] Alif Baa's "arrows to indicate direction of writing" shown directly on the model, every time — not just described once; [waraqaweb Jeem lesson] "body first, dots last" principle |
| 6 | **Positional-forms carousel** — same letter shown isolated / initial / medial / final in one screen, tap through each, one word example per form | Teach the 4 forms attached to THIS letter immediately (Alif Baa model), not deferred to a separate joining unit (Qaida model) — chosen because app users (both children AND adults) benefit from seeing the "why" of the shape change right when the base shape is fresh, and Duolingo's own "different forms of the letters" lesson type is a dedicated screen type, confirming this is viable as a single micro-screen rather than a multi-week deferred lesson | Tap-through carousel, passive + 1 quick matching check | Light check: match 1 form to 1 position-in-word example | [1] Duolingo "different forms" lesson exists as documented feature; [8] Alif Baa shows all forms per letter up front (contrast noted vs. Qaida's fully deferred approach — a DESIGN CHOICE, not a proven-superior one; flagged as such) |
| 7 | **Micro-check / "you do"** — 3-4 mixed items pulling from screens 2–4 with no hints, timed loosely | Independent recall before moving on — the gradual-release "you do" phase | Mixed multiple-choice + 1 trace, no scaffolding cues | ≥75% correct to pass; below that, re-serve screens 2-4 with easier distractors (not the same letter repeated verbatim — Duolingo's mistake-review pattern) | [11] gradual release "you do" independent phase; [12] Duolingo micro-lesson/mini-check norm |

**Length check:** 7 screens × ~20-30 sec/screen ≈ 3–4 minutes, inside the ~5-minute Duolingo lesson
norm [12][2].

## 3. Recommended UNIT Path (Duolingo-style path of short lessons)

Design principle drawn from the 3-way Qaida agreement (isolated → joined → vowel-arc → words → reading)
[3][4][5] blended with Alif Baa's earlier word-introduction and Duolingo's small-batch pacing [2][6]:
**introduce words earlier than Qaida does** (Qaida defers all real words until after the full 5-8
stage vowel arc — a design built for slow, teacher-led madrassa pacing over 3-6 months [5], not a
self-paced app) but **keep the vowel-mark arc as its own explicit lesson type** (not silently folded
into letter lessons) because 3 independent sources treat harakat/tanween/sukoon/shadda as
sequential, separately-mastered skills, not side effects of learning letters [3][4][5].

```
UNIT 1 — First 4 letters (shape-family group, e.g. ا ب ت ث)
  Lesson 1.1  Letter ا  (per-letter flow, §2 above)
  Lesson 1.2  Letter ب
  Lesson 1.3  Letter ت
  Lesson 1.4  Letter ث
  Lesson 1.5  JOINING LESSON — same 4 letters shown connected in pairs/triples
               (mirrors Qaida Lesson 2 "huroof-e-murakkabat" [3], but scoped to only
               the 4 letters just learned, not all 29 at once — a pacing adaptation
               for a self-paced app vs. Qaida's teacher-paced whole-alphabet approach)
  Lesson 1.6  HARAKAT LESSON — fatha/kasra/damma applied ONLY to these 4 letters
               (staged as its own lesson per [3][4][5], but scoped small per-unit
               rather than "all 29 letters get vowels at once")
  Lesson 1.7  FIRST WORD LESSON — 2-3 real short words buildable from these 4
               letters + harakat only (e.g. اب, بت-shape words) — this is the
               earliest word-lesson point that is honestly achievable; going
               earlier would violate every source's requirement that harakat
               come before real reading [3][4][5]
  Lesson 1.8  UNIT CHECK — mixed recall of all 4 letters, their joined forms,
               and harakat, gating progress to Unit 2 (Duolingo-style checkpoint
               [12]; UNVERIFIED exact Duolingo checkpoint mechanics — treat as
               a design borrowing from Duolingo's known general "unit
               checkpoint" concept referenced in course-structure discussions,
               not a confirmed Urdu/Arabic-course-specific screenshot)

UNIT 2 — Next 4 letters (next shape family) → same 1.1-1.8 shape
UNIT 3 → UNIT 7  (continue in shape-family groups of ~4 until all 29-33
   Urdu letters + extra Urdu-specific letters, e.g. ٹ ڈ ڑ ں ھ, are covered —
   Urdu adds letters beyond the 28 Arabic letters, which is OUT OF SCOPE for
   the Arabic-only sources above and should be verified separately against
   an Urdu-specific primer, not assumed identical to Noorani Qaida's 29)

UNIT 8 — TANWEEN LESSON — cross-cutting lesson applying tanween across
   several already-learned letters (per Qaida's L4 staging [3][4][5])

UNIT 9 — MADD / LONG VOWELS LESSON (alif/waw/ye as elongation) [3][5]

UNIT 10 — SUKOON LESSON (consonant with no vowel) [3][5]

UNIT 11 — SHADDA (doubling) LESSON [3][5]

UNIT 12+ — WORD LESSONS AT SCALE — full-vocabulary word-building lessons now
   unlocked by the complete letter+vowel-mark toolkit; this is the point Qaida
   places "combined rules" / real reading [3][4][5]

UNIT 13 — READ — short connected sentences/phrases (Qaida's Lesson 10 /
   Stage 8 "first Mushaf reading" equivalent [3][5], but should be ordinary
   Urdu sentences for this app, not Quranic text, since the app's target
   is general literacy, not Qaida)

UNIT-LEVEL CHECK — gates progress to the next letter-family unit; mirrors
   Duolingo's checkpoint pattern [12] and the Qaida norm of not letting a
   learner proceed without demonstrated mastery of the current stage [3][4][5]
```

**How many letters before the first word lesson:** **4** (one shape-family group), per Unit 1 above.
This is a deliberate compromise: Noorani Qaida defers all words until every one of ~29 letters plus
the full vowel-mark arc is mastered (weeks to months) [3][4][5] — appropriate for a teacher-paced
madrassa setting, but likely to cause the same drop-off Duolingo Arabic alphabet learners report
("I never get new letters," "is Duolingo supposed to be this slow" [7]) if copied verbatim into a
self-paced app. Alif Baa's counter-extreme (vowels + 4 letters in Unit 1, but no readable sentences
until the end of the whole 10-unit book per the review's explicit criticism [8]) shows the opposite
failure mode: introducing letters fast but never delivering the payoff of real words. The 4-letters
→ scoped-joining → scoped-harakat → first-real-word sequence above is designed to give the learner a
genuine "I can read a real word" moment within the first ~35–40 minutes of app use (8 lessons ×
~5 min), which no single source proves optimal — **flag this compromise as a hypothesis to A/B test,
not a cited finding**.

## 5. Key Synthesis Notes (cross-cutting findings)

- **Three independent Qaida sources agree** on the macro-shape: whole-alphabet-first (not
  1-letter-at-a-time) → dedicated joining lesson → a 4-6-lesson staged vowel-mark arc (harakat →
  tanween → madd → sukoon → shadda, in that order across all three sources) → only THEN real
  reading [3][4][5]. This ordering of the *vowel-mark arc itself* is the most solidly corroborated
  finding in this research pass.
- **Alif Baa and Qaida disagree sharply** on when positional forms and vowels enter: Alif Baa
  teaches a letter's forms and relevant vowel marks in the SAME unit as the letter itself [6][8];
  Qaida defers both to dedicated later lessons that apply across the whole alphabet at once [3][4][5].
  Neither source claims to have tested the other approach — this is a real open design decision,
  not a resolved one.
- **Duolingo's specific per-lesson exercise triad (tap-the-sound / build-from-tiles / trace-with-guide)
  is the most directly reusable, app-native pattern found** [2], but Duolingo's own official blog
  posts do not disclose exact letters-per-lesson counts or vowel-introduction timing — those are
  UNVERIFIED gaps in the record confirmed even from the company's own pages.
- **Gradual release ("I do / we do / you do")** [11] is not explicitly named by any Duolingo, Qaida,
  or Alif Baa source, but the per-letter flow in §2 is compatible with it and gives a principled
  reason for the screen ordering (model → scaffolded practice → independent check) beyond just
  copying Duolingo's exercise types.
- **Kitkit School, Feed the Monster (beyond the TPT teacher-marketplace snippet), and Chimple could
  not be verified in this pass** — WebFetch returned empty/blocked content for their primary pages,
  and reddit.com is blocked entirely for WebFetch in this environment. This is a genuine research gap,
  not an assumption — recommend a follow-up pass using app-store listings, YouTube demo/walkthrough
  video descriptions, or a direct app install + screen-recording to fill §1's blank rows.
