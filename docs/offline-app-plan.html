Urdu Reader: an offline app for learners and teachers

اردو پڑھنا سیکھیں، کہیں بھی، بغیر انٹرنیٹ

How to turn the 13-unit research-backed course into one installable app that works with no internet, on a cheap Android phone, for a child, an adult, a teacher with 30 children and one phone, or a parent abroad. Every drill and every test already in the course is kept. Each design choice points to the research file that supports it (all in research/ of the repo).

1 · Who it is for, and what each person needs

Learner, child 5 to 10

  • 10-minute sessions, 2 to 4 bites each
  • Audio first, big Naskh letters, tracing required
  • No streak pressure; a small reward per session
  • No reading of instructions: icons and voice

Learner, adult new to Urdu

  • Same units, faster pace, romanisation shown
  • Tracing optional, dictation required
  • Progress framed as mastery, not competition

Heritage speaker (speaks, cannot read)

  • Placement test skips known ground
  • Meaning drills off, decoding drills on
  • Roman-Urdu scaffold fades after unit 4

Teacher, 30 children, one phone

  • PIN, class roster, instant child switching
  • Scripted 40-minute lesson: I do, we do, you do
  • EGRA test with timer and auto-scoring
  • Class view grouped by reading level, export

Parent or tutor at home

  • One or two child profiles, no PIN
  • Weekly one-page progress slip
  • Guided "sit with your child" mode: the phone says what to do

Sources: research/08_teacher_tools.md §1, §4; research/10_learning_design.md §3, §8; research/07_offline_apps.md (onecourse, Kolibri Coach, Ustad Mobile).

3 · How the app is organised

One app, three modes chosen on first launch, all sharing one content pack and one local database. Kolibri and onecourse both separate the coach view from the learner view and keep the coach view small; we do the same.

  C --> E

Learner mode screens

  1. Today. One button: start the 10-minute session. Below it: the unit map (13 units), the review count due, the last reading speed.
  2. Session. 2 to 4 bites drawn by the engine: due review cards first (Leitner boxes), then the next lesson step, then one fluency read. Ends with a summary and one small reward.
  3. Lesson. The eight steps already in the course: hear, see, tell apart, join, read, write, dictation, check. Steps can be replayed any time.
  4. Review deck. Letters, words and sight words as cards. Missed items go to box 1 and come back tomorrow; correct items move up (1, 2, 4, 8 days). Confusable letters are always interleaved, never blocked.
  5. Read. Sentences and, from unit 7, short decodable passages. Repeated reading with a visible words-per-minute number and a personal best.
  6. Test yourself. The self-administered version of the reading test: letter sounds, nonwords, familiar words with a timer. Framed as "your reading speed", not a certificate.
  7. Progress. Letters mastered (39), words read, speed over time. For children: pictures, not numbers.
  8. Settings. Track, Naskh or Nastaliq, vowel marks on or off, text size and letter spacing, audio-only mode, romanisation on or off.

Teacher mode screens (kept to six)

  1. Class. Roster with a coloured band per child (pre-reader, letters, words, sentences, fluent). Tap a child to open their learner mode. Add child in two taps, no email.
  2. Today's lesson. A 40-minute script for the unit the class is on: I do (10 min, teacher shows the letter cards and plays audio), we do (15 min, chorus reading of the word list), you do (15 min, children rotate through the phone for the tell-apart and dictation drills). Text from course/unit_NN.md.
  3. Groups. Children sorted into the five bands from their latest assessment, with the suggested activity per band (Teaching at the Right Level pattern).
  4. Assess. The EGRA flow, one child at a time, about 8 minutes: letter sounds (60 s), nonwords (60 s), familiar words (60 s), passage (timed, tap each error), 5 comprehension questions. Stop rule: end a subtask after 10 consecutive errors. Scores computed on the spot and mapped to bands (above 90 cwpm exceeds, 60 to 90 meets, under 60 below, 0 nonreader). Big timer, audible stop.
  5. Reports. Class table sortable by speed, a histogram of bands, a one-page parent slip per child, CSV export to a USB drive or by sharing a file. No cloud required.
  6. Content and device. Content pack version, storage used, "keep this device's data" (persistent storage request), teacher PIN change.

Sources: research/08_teacher_tools.md §3 (Tangerine EGRA flow, stop rules), §4 (shared-device model), §5 (must-haves); research/04_pedagogy.md §9 (benchmarks); research/07_offline_apps.md "Minimal teacher dashboard".

Self-administered scores are formative. The evidence for tap-speed as a fluency proxy is thin, so the app must say "your reading speed today", never "grade 2 certified". Only the teacher-run assessment maps to the benchmark bands.

6 · Data, offline storage and sync

  • Content pack (shared by all profiles): letters.json, units.json, 474 Opus clips, two fonts, print PDFs. Versioned with a manifest and per-file hashes. Estimated 35 to 75 MB; the audio alone is about 3 MB.
  • Local database (IndexedDB, one store each): profiles, attempts (every drill answer with timestamp), review cards (box and due date), sessions, assessments (EGRA subtask scores), settings. Deterministic IDs (device id + timestamp + record id) so exports from several phones can be merged without clashes.
  • No account, no server for v1. Backup and sharing by file: export JSON or CSV to the phone's storage, a USB drive, WhatsApp or Bluetooth. Import merges by ID.
  • Later: LAN sync in a school (Kolibri pattern) or QR-code handoff between a child's phone and the teacher's phone.
  • Persistence: request persistent storage after the first full download, and warn the teacher when the device is nearly full. Eviction on a full 16 GB phone is a real risk, not a theoretical one.

Sources: research/09_tech_stack.md §3, §4; research/07_offline_apps.md "Local SQLite + LAN sync", "Modular content bundling".

7 · Technology choice

Progressive Web App first, wrapped for the Play Store with a Trusted Web Activity. Reasons, in order: the whole current course app is already HTML and runs today; Chrome and WebView shape Nastaliq correctly on Android 8 and later; storage quotas cover the pack many times over; one code base serves phone, tablet and a teacher's laptop; Capacitor is available as a fallback if a native API is ever needed. Flutter was rejected because its text engine has known Nastaliq and RTL issues and would force re-writing five working drills.

8 · The audio question, settled honestly

Today's clips are machine-generated with a local Urdu voice, repaired by your listening test. That is right for a pilot. Every successful offline literacy app in the survey (onecourse, Kitkit, Chimple, Curious Learning, the Noorani Qaida apps) ships native-speaker recordings, and the current model's licence is non-commercial. For version 1 the plan is: record one Pakistani voice reading the 474-item manifest (about an hour of studio time), keep the machine clips as a fallback layer, and keep the audio_check page as the review tool for whoever records.

Sources: research/07_offline_apps.md "Pre-recorded narration"; research/05_open_assets.md; research/06_tts_bakeoff.md.

9 · Build plan

Status on 18 September 2026: phases 0 to 2 shipped as v0.1.0 (debug APK on GitHub Releases), including passages, placement, parent report and the self-run five-part test. Phase 3 and the pilot remain.
Phase 0
1 week
Foundations. Split the current single file into modules; content pack loader with versions; IndexedDB schema; profiles; service worker; install on two cheap phones and confirm Opus, fonts and storage persistence. Exit: the existing course runs offline from a Play-installed TWA.
Phase 1
2 weeks
Learner mode. Session engine (review first, then lesson, then read), Leitner deck, interleaved tell-apart, spoken specific feedback, repeated-reading timer, placement test, tracks and accessibility settings, child-friendly progress. Exit: a child can do ten sessions without an adult and the data survives a reboot.
Phase 2
2 weeks
Teacher and parent modes. PIN, roster, instant switching, lesson-of-the-day scripts, EGRA flow with timers and stop rules, bands and groups, parent slip, CSV and JSON export and import. Exit: one teacher assesses five children in 40 minutes and exports a class report on a phone with no internet.
Phase 3
1 week
Content v1. Human voice recording and the printable pack. (Decodable passages for units 7 to 11 shipped in v0.1.) Exit: content pack 1.0 signed and published.
Phase 4
4 weeks
Pilot. Two classrooms plus ten homes; collect attempts and EGRA scores; measure the four-week fluency change; fix what the data says. Exit: a go or no-go on the wider release with numbers, not opinions.

10 · Open decisions for Kamal

  1. Brand and name. Standalone product, a Taleemabad offering, or a Rumi companion? This decides fonts, colours and the Play Store listing.
  2. Recording budget for the human voice, and which voice (a teacher's voice tested with children beats a studio announcer).
  3. Whether the teacher mode is in the same APK (simpler) or a second app (cleaner store listing). The plan assumes one APK with a device-type choice.
  4. Pilot partners: which two schools and which diaspora families.
  5. Whether to keep Roman-Urdu scaffolding for heritage adults or drop it entirely; the evidence is course-description level, not trial level.

11 · What this plan does not claim

  • Nastaliq stroke-order pedagogy has no published evidence base; the tracer stays at start-side, coverage and stroke count until someone records real stroke data.
  • Naskh-first ordering, heritage-learner speed-up and the 10-minute session length are reasoned inferences from adjacent research, not Urdu trials. The pilot in phase 4 is where they get tested.
  • Self-administered fluency numbers are directional only.
  • Several tech-stack facts (Opus on specific WebViews, persistent storage on full devices) are marked UNVERIFIED in the research until tested on real phones.
